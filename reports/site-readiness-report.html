Why it matters Google decides what to rank based on what it can read. Before these fixes, crawlers saw three pages, a missing sitemap and a garbled headline. Now they see a complete, clearly structured site — the baseline every ranking is built on.

Built to convert

Getting found is half the job. Here's the other half.

The six systems above bring qualified Massachusetts families to the site. Everything below is how the site turns those visitors into booked reviews — each decision grounded in established conversion and behavioral research.

07 One funnel, one ask per page

Every page has exactly one primary action, matched to how ready the visitor is.

  • Guides → Calculator → Review — a reader arrives from search, sees their own estate-tax number, then books. Each step is the natural next one, never a leap
  • CTA hierarchy on every page — one gold primary action; anything else is visibly secondary. Guide pages lead with the calculator; the calculator leads with the review
  • Competing asks removed — no duplicate forms, no exit links at decision moments, no offers fighting each other for the same click
Why it matters Decision research is unambiguous: every additional choice on a page slows the visitor down and increases abandonment (Hick's Law, the paradox of choice). A page that asks for one thing gets it more often than a page that asks for three.

08 The calculator as conversion engine

The single most persuasive asset on the site is the visitor's own number.

  • A 60-second, no-commitment first step — far easier to say yes to than a 45-minute call, which is exactly why it's the site-wide primary ask for cold visitors
  • Abstract tax law becomes a personal dollar figure — "$180,800" lands harder than any paragraph about M.G.L. c. 65C
  • The booking ask arrives at peak motivation — the moment their number is on screen: "Walk Through My Number: 45 Min, Free"
Why it matters People are roughly twice as motivated to avoid a loss as to chase a gain (loss aversion). A visitor who has just seen what Massachusetts would take from their family is in a different frame of mind than one reading generic marketing — and small first commitments reliably lead to larger ones (the foot-in-the-door effect).

09 A booking path with no leaks

When a visitor decides to act, nothing stands between them and the request.

  • Five-field form, about 60 seconds — name, contact and one qualifying question. Scheduling details belong on the call, not the form
  • The asset question keeps the calendar clean — every booked review is a genuine $2M+ prospect, so Michael's time goes to real fits
  • The review's deliverables are spelled out in writing — four concrete things every caller walks away with, which removes the fear of a sales ambush
  • A phone number backstop — time-sensitive prospects can skip the form entirely
Why it matters Form length is one of the most consistent conversion levers in the industry — every unnecessary field costs real submissions. And for a high-trust purchase like financial advice, spelling out exactly what happens on the call (and what doesn't) is what converts hesitation into a booked slot.

10 The persuasion details, line by line

Beyond the structure, the copy itself applies specific, research-backed conversion techniques.

  • Risk reversal at the ask — "no cost, no obligation," "no products to sell," and the promise of gaps named in writing "whether or not we ever speak again." Every reason to fear the call is answered before it's booked
  • Anchoring and contrast — the calculator shows the before-planning tax next to the with-planning scenario, so the value of acting is a visible dollar gap, not an abstract claim
  • Specific numbers over adjectives — "$82,400 on a $3M estate," "a $99,600 credit," "45 minutes," "60 seconds." Specificity reads as competence; vagueness reads as marketing
  • Objection-handling FAQ — "Is Michael an attorney?", "Do I need $2 million?" The questions a hesitant prospect is silently asking are answered on the page, in plain language
  • CTAs that say what you get — "Calculate My Estate Tax," "Walk Through My Number: 45 Min, Free" — never a generic "Submit" or "Learn More"
  • A page that argues in order — problem → personal number → solution → proof → fit → close. Classic attention-interest-desire-action structure, one act at a time
Why it matters Structure gets a visitor to the right place; the words decide what they do there. Each of these is a documented persuasion lever — risk reversal defuses regret, anchors make value concrete, and specificity builds the credibility a $2M+ household requires before handing over a phone number.

11 Trust signals that do the selling

Affluent families don't respond to pressure. The site persuades the way a fiduciary would.

  • "Not for everyone" positioning — the fit section names exactly who the practice serves. The right families feel chosen; the wrong ones self-select out before they cost you a call
  • The readiness checklist — visitors audit their own plan and watch the gaps appear. They build the case for the review themselves, which is far more convincing than being told
  • Generosity before any ask — seven full guides and the calculator, free, no email wall. Give real value first and people are naturally inclined to reciprocate
  • Credentials at every decision point — CFP®, CRD #107768, SEC verification links, fiduciary and no-product language, ~$1.5B managed since 1997
Why it matters These are the classic levers of ethical persuasion — reciprocity, authority, commitment and consistency — applied the way a $2M+ household expects to be treated. Exclusivity attracts the affluent buyer; free expertise builds the obligation to engage; and a self-scored checklist turns a skeptical reader into someone who has already argued your side.
